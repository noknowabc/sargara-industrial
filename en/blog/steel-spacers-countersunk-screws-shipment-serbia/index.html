Steel Spacers and M5 x 90 Countersunk Screws Shipped to Serbia

A Serbia shipment case covering two labeled steel spacer variants, an M5 x 90 countersunk screw, order packing, palletizing and container loading from China.

Cartons of steel spacers and countersunk screws grouped on export pallets before container loading for Serbia.

This order brought together two steel spacer variants and a longer countersunk screw for a customer in Serbia. SARGARA coordinated the China-side purchase and shipment. The photos follow the order from labeled inner packs and cartons through pallet preparation and loading into a container.

The parts are small; keeping them identifiable is the important part. Separate item labels, bag quantities and outer-carton markings make it easier for the receiving team to connect each package with the purchase list instead of sorting a mixed box of similar-looking hardware.

Two spacer variants, kept identifiable

The cartons contain two short cylindrical spacer components. The labels distinguish them as Distancer 1 and Distancer 2, and show compact dimensional callouts together with an M4 reference. One outer label records eight bags of 1,000 pieces, for a stated total of 8,000 pieces. The other item is shown in labeled inner bags, with 1,000 pieces indicated per bag.

Both spacer labels show 10B21 in the material field. The printed dimension strings are abbreviated, so they should not replace a controlled drawing or approved sample. For a repeat order, the buyer and supplier should confirm the outside diameter, overall length, bore or thread details, and tolerances against the signed specification before production.

The second fastener in the order is a countersunk screw measuring M5 x 90 mm, with a 55 mm rolled-thread section. That length and thread detail came from the purchasing information provided for this case. The available photos do not establish the screw's material grade, drive recess, pitch or countersunk angle, so those details should remain tied to the buyer's drawing rather than inferred from a photograph.

Packing the order for shipment

The order was packed in stages. Labeled bags sit inside cartons, and an outer label identifies the spacer item and the packed quantity. A separate photo records the long screws beside a weighing scale during the packing process. The display is not clear enough to quote a reliable weight, so no weight figure is used here.

Once the cartons were closed, they were grouped on wooden supports and wrapped for warehouse handling. The pallet photographs show the cartons arranged together before dispatch. This matters with a mixed hardware order: keeping the cartons grouped helps maintain item separation while making the freight easier to move as one load.

What buyers should confirm on a spacer and screw RFQ

For custom steel spacers, include a drawing or sample, the material grade, critical diameters and lengths, bore or thread details, tolerance, finish and required quantity. If several spacer variants look alike, assign each one a distinct item number and state the bag quantity and carton label format.

For a countersunk screw, specify the complete size, how the length is measured, thread pitch and threaded length, head angle and dimensions, drive type, material and finish. A description such as “M5 x 90 countersunk screw” is a useful start, but it may not be enough to prevent a mismatch in assembly.

Also agree on packing before the order is released: pieces per bag, bags per carton, labels, carton count, pallet or crate requirements and destination marks. If goods from more than one supplier are involved, confirm whether the shipment should be consolidated and how each item will remain traceable after repacking.
