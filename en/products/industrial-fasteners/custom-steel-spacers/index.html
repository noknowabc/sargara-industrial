Product Overview

Custom steel spacers are made to hold a defined gap, offset or mounting distance in an assembly. Their fit depends on details such as outside diameter, body length, bore, thread and end geometry. Where a standard part does not match the assembly, a controlled drawing or approved sample gives suppliers a clear basis for quotation.

A related long countersunk screw uses an M5 x 90 mm specification and a 55 mm rolled thread. Because these are separate part types, each needs its own drawing callouts, material requirement and inspection criteria.

Where Spacers and Countersunk Screws Are Used

A spacer is useful wherever two parts need a consistent separation or mounting offset. Typical assemblies include equipment panels, brackets, small machinery, instrument housings and fixtures. A countersunk screw can sit flush when its head angle matches the prepared recess in the mating component.

Selection should follow the joint design. The correct spacer length, bore or thread must match the fastener and surrounding components; the countersunk head must also match the recess rather than being chosen by appearance alone.

Details to Confirm Before Ordering

  • Outside diameter, body length, bore and thread form for each spacer
  • Thread size, pitch and engagement depth where the spacer is threaded
  • M5 thread pitch, screw length datum and 55 mm threaded-section requirement
  • Countersunk angle, head dimensions and drive recess for the screw
  • Steel grade, mechanical requirement and surface finish for each item
  • Critical tolerances, inspection method, quantity and packing specification

Steel Spacer and Countersunk Screw FAQ

What is a steel spacer used for?

A steel spacer holds a defined distance between assembled parts. Depending on its bore, thread and profile, it can support a fastener, set clearance or position a component. The mating parts and drawing determine the correct configuration.

Are the two spacer variants threaded?

The supplied labels use compact M4 callouts, but they do not fully explain the thread form or engagement depth. Confirm those details against the controlled drawing before quoting a repeat order.

What does M5 x 90 mean for the countersunk screw?

It identifies an M5 nominal thread and a 90 mm screw length as specified for this item. The drawing or selected standard should define how length is measured for the countersunk head, along with pitch, drive and head angle.

Is 10B21 the material for the M5 screw as well?

The 10B21 marking appears on the steel spacer order labels. The screw material was not specified in the supplied information, so it should be stated separately in the drawing or purchase specification.
